# Loop record — OODA terminology

The design of the OODA vocabulary's use in Waytide: whether **orientation** and **action** serve as the generalized covering terms for the two mode-dependent planning artifacts, and whether a third **OODA mode** may use the same two words concretely.

**Mode: attended.** Chosen at initiation beside the working location. The loop gates at every hinge and the developer deliberates each one.

---

## Cycle 1 — whether a covering term may be one of the things it covers

**Hinge.** Today's covering terms are neutral: no mode's concrete artifact is called a direction artifact or a sequencing artifact. The proposal puts the same two words in both places, which the forecast called a structural defect — a term ambiguous between the class and its member. Everything downstream rests on this, since it decides whether the generalization and the third mode can share a vocabulary.

**Options.**

1. **Same words, declared deliberately** — orientation and action are both the covering terms and OODA mode's concrete artifacts, the ambiguity accepted and stated outright.
2. **OODA words cover; the mode uses others** — the covering terms are orientation and action, and the third mode takes different concrete words, keeping every mode's artifacts distinct from the class name.
3. **OODA words cover; no third mode** — the OODA vocabulary lives at the general layer alone.
4. **Keep direction and sequencing; OODA is a mode** — the covering terms stand as settled earlier the same day, and the OODA words are the third mode's concrete artifacts only.

**Decision.** Option 1 — **the same words, declared deliberately.**

**What the decision does to the forecast's problem 5.** It does not accept the ambiguity; it **dissolves it**, by inverting the relation the forecast assumed. The forecast treated the covering terms as a neutral layer above three peer modes, in which case a term shared with one mode is ambiguous. Under this decision **OODA mode is the unrenamed case** — its vocabulary *is* the system's general vocabulary, and formal and intuitive are the two modes that **rename**. A covering term shared with OODA mode is then not a class name colliding with a member; it is the base term, and the other two modes are departures from it.

That is a stronger position than any of the alternatives offered, and it was not among the options the agent produced — the option set framed the question as how to *avoid* the collision, and the developer answered by removing the ground it stood on.

## Cycle 2 — whether *action* names the sequencing artifact

**Hinge.** The forecast's problem 4 held that *action* mis-names its artifact, an action being an event where a file is not one. Two things bore on the decision, and the agent surfaced both.

**The forecast's own objection is weaker than it recorded.** *Aspiration* and *intention* are not documents either, and both are established artifact names here. An artifact named for what it states is already this system's practice, so *an action* is no worse on that count than *an intention*.

**What replaced it is sharper.** In the DBE vocabulary's own OODA table the **human owns Orient and Decide**; the agent owns Observe and Act. The planning artifacts are the human's. So *orientation / action* pairs one human phase with one agent phase, where *orientation / decision* would take the human's two — and the weight of that rose with cycle 1, since the base vocabulary is now the one the whole system reads through.

**Options.**

1. **action** — pairs with orientation on the near/far axis the mode rule already states, and the doing is what a sequencing artifact sequences. Costs the phase fidelity.
2. **decision** — the human's own two phases, and a state a file can record. Collides with the decision log in `waytide/local/log/`.

**Decision.** **action.**

## Cycle 3 — a hinge put and withdrawn

**Hinge as put.** How the artifact sense and the DBE loop-phase sense of *orientation* and *action* are distinguished. The agent offered three options — the class noun as a mandatory disambiguator, a scoped ambiguity on the `cycle` precedent, or DBE yielding the bare noun forms.

**Withdrawn, and the withdrawal is the record.** The developer said three times, across an explanation, a re-explanation, and a summary, that it was not clear what was being asked. That was not a failure to follow: **it was not a hinge.**

**Two reasons, either sufficient.**

- **The system already runs words with several senses, routinely.** *Design* is the activity, the document, and half the method's name. The DBE vocabulary states outright that *cycle* carries three nested meanings scoped by the word before it, and calls that deliberate. Context resolves them, as it always has. A word carrying a phase sense and an artifact sense is ordinary here, not an exception needing a ruling.
- **The convention that would settle it was already settled.** Earlier the same day, in `b5d2ad8`, the covering terms were required to keep the trailing class noun. *The orientation artifact* is a file; bare *orientation* is the phase. The answer was in the rule before the question was put.

**What the agent got wrong.** The forecast's problem 3 called this the central difficulty and predicted it would prove worse than it looked. It was the reverse — the problem was smaller than it looked, and the forecast's own point 9 said as much and was not weighted. Gating it was ceremony: subtle, perhaps, but not load-bearing, which the DBE vocabulary names as the case where a gate should not fire.

**Nothing was decided here**, and nothing needed to be.

## Cycle 4 — the reconsideration, and the verdict

**Hinge.** Not put by the agent. The developer reconsidered the proposal as a whole: keep *direction* and *sequencing* as the abstract covering terms, and have an OODA mode.

**That is hinge 1's option 4**, offered at the first gate and declined there in favor of option 1. The option set was complete; what had changed was how much of the design was in view.

**Decision.** The covering terms stand as `b5d2ad8` settled them — **the direction artifact** and **the sequencing artifact**. The OODA words become the third mode's concrete artifacts and nothing more.

**The reasoning, as the developer gave it.** *Direction* and *sequencing* are the better **abstract** terms. That is the axis none of the agent's four options was framed on — every option asked how the general layer and the mode should **relate**, and none asked which words are more **abstract**, which is the property a covering term is actually judged on.

**Verdict.** The experiment is **refuted**: orientation and action do not serve as the generalized covering terms. Declared through the selection interface, with inconclusive offered and declined.

**What follows.** The OODA mode proceeds as a **feature** — built to an intent, with no question under test — and is not part of this experiment.

---

## What this loop record shows about the loop

Three of its four cycles are worth reading for the method rather than the outcome:

- **Cycle 1 was answered in a way the option set did not contain** — the developer inverted the relation rather than choosing among the arrangements offered.
- **Cycle 3 should not have been put.** It was subtle and not load-bearing, which the DBE vocabulary names as ceremony.
- **Cycle 4 reversed cycle 1** with an argument on an axis no gate had raised.

The pattern common to all three: the agent's option sets framed the questions on the relations it could see, and the decisive reasoning each time came from somewhere the framing did not reach. That is the two results the gates rest on, working as intended — the best options are ones the agent cannot produce — and it is also an argument for putting fewer, later gates.
