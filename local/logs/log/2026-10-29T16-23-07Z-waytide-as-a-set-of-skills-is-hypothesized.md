# Waytide as a set of skills is hypothesized — the command-triggered rules as skills, the rules always in force kept in the session-start read

**Tags:** [hypothesis]

This is a hypothesis. Nothing was built and nothing was decided.

A skill loads only when the agent judges that the task matches its description. That is the judgment the announce-waytide-at-session-start rule exists to remove, so a rule whose force depends on being always present cannot be a skill.

1. **Always in force, and kept in the read:** the `language` rules, the vocabularies, present-every-prompt, ask-at-once, no-claude-coauthor, the commit-message rules, and journal-is-read-at-session-start. A vocabulary cannot be a skill, since its terms bind every sentence and the order of the vocabularies needs all of them read.
2. **Triggered by a command, and fit for skills:** the status report, the test report, the test tree, the timeline view, *new entry*, the private journal commands, *begin/end/resume collecting*, and *Waytide reserves the word, X*.
3. **Triggered by a condition, and the uncertain group:** the start of an implementation, a release, a branch switch, a publish, and a work session record. A skill can hold the procedure, but the agent must recognize the condition, and a missed trigger leaves no trace.

The hypothesis gains a smaller session-start read, commands an engineer can find as slash commands, and scripts carried with their skill. It costs the read-and-withheld guarantee of the package-set rule, delivery by `git subtree`, and the portability of `AGENTS.md` to other harnesses.

The test that would refute it moves the command-triggered rules alone into skills. It asks two questions. Does each command still produce what its rule specifies, where the skill is the only copy of the rule? Does a rule that is always in force go unapplied inside a command's procedure? The condition-triggered rules are a separate, later test.
