# Loop record — The OODA mode

The design of a third project mode, **OODA**, whose planning artifacts are an **orientation** and
an **action**. The covering terms are not in scope — they stand as `b5d2ad8` settled them.

**Mode: attended.** Chosen at initiation beside the working location. The loop gates at every
hinge and the developer deliberates each one.

**A correction carried from the experiment that preceded this.** That loop put a hinge before it
was ripe and gated one thing that was not a hinge at all. The correction is fewer gates, put
later — not none, and not a lower standard for what counts as a hinge.

---

## Cycle 1 — what the mode rule is called

**Hinge.** The rule's filename is `a-project-works-in-formal-or-intuitive-mode`, which a third mode
makes false. Renaming reconciles 13 citations across 9 files. The trade is the one the system
worked through this morning from the other side: enumerate and be self-evident, or generalize and
survive the next change.

**Options.**

1. **`a-project-works-in-a-mode-chosen-at-initiation`** — names what binds, without counting or
   listing. Survives a fourth mode. Costs the self-evidence of reading the modes off the filename.
2. **`a-project-works-in-formal-intuitive-or-ooda-mode`** — enumerates, as the current name does.
   Legible at a glance; breaks again on a fourth mode.
3. **`a-project-works-in-one-of-three-modes`** — counts rather than lists. Breaks on a fourth mode,
   and a count is the least informative thing the name could carry.

**Decision.** Option 1 — **`a-project-works-in-a-mode-chosen-at-initiation`**.

**Consistent with the covering-term reasoning already in this rule.** The rule argues that an
installed rule carrying concrete names goes stale the moment Waytide changes them, and that fewer
specifics means less that can rot between refreshes. A filename enumerating the modes is that same
specificity, in the one place every citing rule has to repeat it.

**What was done.** The file renamed, its title changed to *A project works in a mode, chosen at its
initiation*, and all 13 citations reconciled — 8 rule files, the foundation README, and
`install.sh`. Behavior-neutral: no claim in the rule changed, and the body still describes two
modes, which remains true until the third is added.

## Cycle 2 — the mode written into the rules, ungated

**Hinge — none. Not gated, and deliberately so.** Two questions came up in this cycle and neither
was a hinge:

- **Where Observe and Decide went.** A mode named for OODA that names two artifacts invites the
  question. It is **subtle** — how to frame the mode's relation to the loop takes judgment — but
  it is **not load-bearing**: it is a paragraph in one rule, nothing rests on it, and changing it
  later is cheap. Subtle without load-bearing is what the DBE vocabulary calls ceremony, and
  gating it would have repeated the experiment's cycle 3 exactly.
- **The two directory names.** `orientation/` and `action/`, named for their artifacts, as every
  existing planning directory is. Readable off the pattern, so crude rather than subtle.

**What was decided, and the reasoning applied.** The rule now answers the four-phase question
outright: neither Observe nor Decide produces a **planning** artifact. Observe is the agent
surfacing state, which is shown rather than written down. Decide happens at a gate and *is*
recorded — in the decision log and in loop records — but those are not planning artifacts and do
not vary by mode. So the mode takes the loop's two poles of intent and leaves its other two phases
where they already live. The rule says so, rather than leaving a reader to work it out.

**One thing the rule states because it would otherwise be misread.** The pair straddles the
human/agent split — Orient is the human's phase, Act is the agent's — and the rule says this is
deliberate, since the pairing is on the near/far axis every mode shares rather than on who
performs the phase.

**What was built.** The mode rule extended to three modes throughout — the table row, the near/far
sentence, the local-rule template's third fill, the corroboration section generalized off the two
named modes, and a new section on the OODA vocabulary. The five rules that are *about* the
artifacts now name the OODA pair beside the other two, per this rule's own requirement that a rule
about an artifact names every mode's. The foundation README updated. The mode selection at
initiation becomes three options, formal still the default.

**A defect found and corrected in passing.** Two commits in this feature appended provenance lines
carrying the same hardcoded timestamp, producing two identical footer lines in five files — the
precise collision the a-time-value-carries-minutes-and-seconds rule exists to prevent, arriving
because the agent reused a fixed string rather than reading the clock. Corrected to the real time.

## Outcome

**The mode is built and the feature is completed.** Three modes where there were two: formal,
intuitive, and OODA, whose artifacts are an orientation in `waytide/local/orientation/` and an
action in `waytide/local/action/`. The mode selection at initiation becomes three options with
formal still the default. Nine files changed across two cycles.

Verified before integrating — no suite exists here, so what was confirmed is that the old rule
name appears nowhere, no two-mode phrasing remains in `system/` outside design-by-efferent's
unrelated attended/unattended pair, each artifact rule names the OODA pair, no file carries
duplicate provenance lines, and every shell script parses. Merged to `master`.

## What this loop shows about the loop

**Two cycles, one gate.** The experiment that preceded this feature gated four times in a loop of
comparable size, and two of those gates were faults — one hinge put before it was ripe, one thing
gated that was not a hinge at all. This loop put a single gate, on the one decision that was both
subtle and load-bearing, and generated through the rest.

**The correction was not "gate less".** It was to apply the test the vocabulary already states:
subtle **and** load-bearing. The four-phase question passed the first and failed the second, so it
was decided and written rather than put. The directory names failed the first, being readable off
the pattern every planning directory already follows. Neither is a lowered standard; both are the
standard, applied.

**What the single gate bought.** The rule's name was the one decision that would have been
expensive to reverse — 13 citations reconciled, and every citing rule repeating whatever was
chosen. That is load-bearing in the literal sense the vocabulary gives it: cheap now, costly once
things rest on it.
