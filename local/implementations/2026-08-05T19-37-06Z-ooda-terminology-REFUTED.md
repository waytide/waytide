# Experiment — OODA terminology

**Tags:** [experiment]

## Question

Do **orientation** and **action** serve as the generalized covering terms for the two
mode-dependent planning artifacts — design/aspiration and plan/intention — given that OODA is
already load-bearing in Waytide through the design-by-efferent vocabulary?

A second part is in view and is not separable from the first: a **third mode** is intended, an
**OODA mode**, whose concrete artifacts would use the same two words. The question is therefore
also whether a covering term may be identical to one mode's concrete term.

## Forecast

Committed before the work.

### The foundation is real, and it is the strongest thing here

**1. OODA is not an import.** The design-by-efferent vocabulary carries a full section on the loop
as a distributed OODA cycle, assigns Observe and Act to the agent and Orient and Decide to the
human, and states that Orient dominates. Terms drawn from there are drawn from the system's own
vocabulary rather than borrowed. I forecast this holds up entirely and is the proposal's best
argument.

**2. The near/far axis survives.** *Orientation* answers *what are we going toward* and *action*
answers *what are we going to do*, which is the pairing the mode rule already states. No mismatch
here.

### Three problems I expect to find

**3. The words already mean something else in this system, and the two meanings are not
compatible.** In the DBE vocabulary **Orient** is a *phase of a loop* owned by the human and
occupied for a moment at a gate; **Act** is the agent generating within the cradle. Under the
proposal the same words also name *classes of file*. A phase and a document are different kinds of
thing, and the language package's own premise says a term meaning one thing here and another there
is worse than either. **I forecast this is the central difficulty** and that it is worse than it
first looks, because both senses are live in the same rules.

**4. *Action* mis-names its artifact, and *orientation* does not.** A document that sequences work
is not an action; it is a statement of what actions will be taken. *Orientation* names a **state**,
and a state can be recorded in a file. *Action* names an **event**, and a file is not one. Compare
the four concrete terms, all of which pass this test: a *design*, an *aspiration*, a *plan*, and an
*intention* are each a thing a document can be. I forecast this is the sharpest single objection,
and that it applies to *action* alone.

**5. A covering term must not be one of the things it covers.** Today the covering terms are
neutral: no mode's concrete artifact is called a direction artifact or a sequencing artifact. Under
the proposal, OODA mode's concrete terms would **be** the covering terms, so *the orientation
artifact* would be ambiguous between the general class and one mode's member of it. I forecast this
is a structural defect rather than a matter of taste, and that it is the part the proposal has not
yet accounted for.

### On the class noun

**6. The trailing `artifact` still has to hold, and will be harder to hold here.** Settled hours
earlier: a covering term keeps its class noun so it cannot become a word the work is done in. Bare
*orientation* and *action* are more sayable than bare *direction* and *sequencing*, not less, so
the constraint matters more. I forecast the terms have to be *the orientation artifact* and *the
action artifact*, and that the pull toward dropping the noun will be stronger than before.

### On the third mode

**7. Two phases of four will read as an omission.** A mode called OODA mode that names two
artifacts leaves Observe and Decide unrepresented, and a reader who knows OODA will look for them.
I forecast this needs an answer — either that the mode names only the two phases that produce
artifacts, or that the mode is named for something other than the whole loop.

**8. The mode itself is sound, separately from the covering-term use.** An OODA mode whose
artifacts are an orientation and an action is a coherent third vocabulary alongside formal and
intuitive, and problem 3 does not reach it as hard, because within one mode the artifact sense
would be the local one. I forecast the proposal splits: **the mode is stronger than the
generalization**.

### Predicted verdict

**Inconclusive**, resolving into a split rather than a single answer: the OODA **mode** affirmed in
substance, the OODA words as **covering terms** refuted on problems 3 and 5, and *action* refuted
on problem 4 independent of either. I expect the experiment's value to be in separating the two
uses, which the proposal currently joins.

**9. One thing I expect to be wrong about**, stated so the miss is legible: I may be overweighting
problem 3. The system already lets *cycle* carry three scoped meanings deliberately, and says so in
the DBE vocabulary — so a term carrying a phase sense and an artifact sense may be admissible on
the same grounds, with the qualifier doing the work. If that is right, problem 3 weakens
considerably and the verdict moves toward affirmed.

### The verdict, and what refuted the question

**Refuted.** *Orientation* and *action* do not serve as the generalized covering terms. **The
direction artifact** and **the sequencing artifact** stand, as settled earlier the same day in
`b5d2ad8`.

What refuted it was not any of the three problems the forecast raised. It was the developer's
judgment, on reconsideration, that *direction* and *sequencing* are the better **abstract**
terms — which is the axis the forecast never put a point on. The three problems were each
answered or dissolved before that judgment arrived, and none of them was load-bearing.

### The finding: the proposal was two proposals

Forecast point 8 predicted the split and was right. The proposal joined a **generalization** and a
**mode**, and they had different merits:

- **As covering terms, the OODA words lose to what they would replace.** A covering term is read
  by someone deciding what a rule governs; abstraction is the property that matters, and
  *direction* and *sequencing* are more abstract than *orientation* and *action*, which carry a
  specific method's framing into rules that have nothing to do with that method.
- **As a mode's concrete terms, the OODA words are sound**, and the mode survives the experiment
  intact. It proceeds as a **feature**, since it is built to an intent and no question is being
  tested.

### The miss: a hinge was put before it was ripe

**Not forecast at all, and it is the most transferable thing here.**

Hinge 1 asked how the covering terms and the mode's concrete terms relate, and offered four
options. The developer took option 1. Two cycles later, with more of the design in view, the
answer became **option 4** — an option that had been offered and declined.

The option set was not wrong; the **timing** was. The hinge was put at the first moment it could
be articulated rather than the first moment it could be answered, and answering it early fixed a
relation that later reasoning had to unwind. A hinge asked before its consequences are visible
gets an answer that does not survive them.

### The second miss: a non-hinge was gated

Cycle 3 put a question that was not a hinge — how the artifact sense and the loop-phase sense are
distinguished — and it cost an explanation, a re-explanation, and a summary before the agent
recognized the fault was in the question. The answer already existed in the class-noun convention
settled hours earlier, and the system already carries multi-sense words routinely (*design*,
*cycle*). The DBE vocabulary names this case exactly: subtle but not load-bearing is **ceremony**,
and gating it is the failure the method exists to retire.

## Findings

- **The generalization and the mode are separable, and separating them is what the experiment
  produced.** The proposal's two halves had different merits and the joined form obscured it.
- **Abstraction is the property a covering term is judged on.** A term carrying a specific
  method's framing is less abstract than one that does not, whatever the method's standing in the
  system.
- **The OODA mode survives and proceeds as a feature**, built to an intent rather than tested as
  a question.
- **A hinge can be put too early.** The option set may be complete and the answer still not
  survive the reasoning that follows it.
- **The agent gated a non-hinge and did not notice for three exchanges.** Repeated failure to
  understand a question is evidence about the question.
