# ASD-STE100 allows project-specific words to be added, which is the accommodation Waytide's coined terms need

The AeroSpace and Defence Simplified Technical English specification restricts writing to an
approved dictionary, and **permits a project to approve its own technical names and technical
verbs** for its domain. Waytide's settled terms are exactly that case.

- **Gated on:** the Simplified Technical English experiment reaching a verdict. Approving a word
  list presumes the specification is adopted, and that is what the experiment tests.

## Why this changes the forecast it answers

The experiment's forecast holds that **the approved dictionary is where the specification breaks**,
because the system's coined terms are its conveyable output and none is ordinary English. The
project-word provision is the direct answer to that, and it moves the question from *can these
words be used at all* to **which of them are technical names, which are technical verbs, and which
are neither**.

## The terms this would have to account for

Read from the packages' `vocabulary.md` files, which already serve as approved dictionaries:

- **design-by-efferent** — a cycle, the hinge cycle, hinge, gate, deliberation, mechanical, subtle,
  cradle, efferent, actuation, observation, load-bearing, solubility, mean-bias, origination,
  attended, unattended.
- **foundation** — deferred, suspended, out of scope, projection, reconcile, working-state artifact,
  recognizable-content line, work session record.
- **language** — solubility, efferent, actuation, and the substitution table's *say* column.
- **testing** — controls, precondition, verified, protect.
- **versioning** — product generation.

**Two properties make this harder than a word list.** Several terms are **verbs used as nouns**, or
the reverse — *actuation* is the noun for an act the system declines to call *the call*, and
*mediates* is required as a bare verb. And several are **ordinary English words held to a narrower
meaning** — *controls*, *protect*, *verified*, *deferred*, *suspended*. A specification that gives
each word one meaning and one part of speech has to be told which meaning, and the substitution
tables are where that is already written down.

**Why:** the forecast named the dictionary as the breaking point and named the technical-name
provision as the obvious accommodation without testing whether it stretches to a methodology's own
coinages. This item is that test, and it is the difference between the specification being
inapplicable to Waytide and merely requiring work.

**How to apply:** wait for the experiment's verdict. If the specification is adopted in any part,
enumerate the terms from each `vocabulary.md`, classify each as a technical name, a technical
verb, or an ordinary word held to one sense, and record the result where the vocabularies already
are rather than in a new list. Related: the Simplified Technical English experiment (which this is
gated on), each package's `vocabulary.md` (the dictionaries this would formalize), and the
`language` package's substitutions table (where the one-meaning constraint is already recorded).
