## Question

**What does the ASD-STE100 specification say?**

**The question is descriptive, and a forecast is what makes it testable.** A descriptive question
reaches no verdict on its own. This record forecasts the specification's structure and content
before the document is read. The gap between the forecast and the document is the finding. That
gap is what the verdict is about.

### The question widened, and the experiment was renamed — 2026-08-10

**The experiment outgrew the document.** Reading the specification was answered, and the work that

2. **How does Waytide use STE, and what does using it change about Waytide's own language?** This

**How the STE dictionary marks a word.** An approved word is in capitals with a definition. A word
that is not approved is in lower case with the approved word that replaces it. A word can also be
absent. STE Rule 1.1 permits an absent word only as a technical noun or a technical verb.

**`gate` is settled as a noun and replaced as a verb.** It is absent from the STE dictionary, so
STE Rule 1.5 admits it as a technical noun in category 19. STE Rule 1.7 forbids that noun as a verb, and
the verb form is replaced by WAIT. The mechanism keeps the name it has in 97 places; the 35 verb
uses change.

**WAIT is the approved word because the stop at a hinge is temporary.** The STE dictionary gives WAIT
the meaning *to stop doing something while another thing occurs*, which is what the loop does at a
hinge — it waits while the human deliberates and decides, and it resumes on the decision. STOP is
approved only in terminal meanings, *to cause the end of a procedure, movement, or operation* and
*to come to an end*, so STE Rule 1.3 rules it out here. Two verb sites keep a different word, because
what they describe is terminal rather than temporary: `nothing gates the merge` takes PREVENT, and
`the working location is gated rather than fixed` takes SELECT.

**The sweep was carried out on 2026-08-08**, across twenty files. Every verb use under `system/` is
replaced, and every remaining `gates` is the plural noun. A third terminal site was found in the
sweep: the `versioning` rule's `the decision is gated rather than proposed and confirmed` takes the
noun, `put at a gate`.

**Two written marks changed, and each carries a debt to be settled when this branch merges.**

- **The loop record's `"none — not gated"` is now `"none — no gate"`**, using the settled noun.

- **The deferred item's `**Gated on:**` field is now `**Waits for:**`.** The developer settled this

### `gating cadence` is retired for `the human's level of participation`

`gating cadence` named the attended-or-unattended choice at six places in `system/`.

**The specification permits the phrase.** **STE Rule 3.5** governs the `-ing` form: *Use the
"-ing" form of a verb only as a technical noun or as a modifier in a technical noun.* Its examples
are `switching relay`, `grinding wheel`, `polishing disc`, and `welding torch` — a modifier formed
from a verb, in front of the noun it qualifies. `gating cadence` is that shape, so it was
admissible.

**The agent first cited STE Rule 1.7 against the phrase, and that was wrong.** STE Rule 1.7 forbids
a technical noun used as a verb, which reaches `the loop gates at hinges` and not an `-ing`
modifier. STE Rule 3.5 is the governing rule for `-ing` forms and permits it. The error was found
when a cheat sheet supplied on 2026-08-08 claimed *no -ing forms as verbs or nouns*, which sent the
check to STE Rule 3.5 — the cheat sheet is wrong about the specification and right about there
being something to check.

**The phrase is retired on the meaning, not on the specification.** Attended and unattended cause a
cadence; they are not one. What they name is a quality of the human's involvement in the loop —
whether the human is in it. The choice is binary and fixed for the work's duration, so nothing
recurs at an interval.

**`participation` is the replacement**, written as **`the human's level of participation`**. It is absent
from the STE dictionary, so STE Rule 1.5 admits it as a technical noun in category 19. `human` is likewise
absent, and the two-word noun is inside STE Rule 2.1's limit of three words.

### `discontinue` and `reserve` are reserved on 2026-08-08

**`discontinue` is a disagreement with the STE dictionary rather than a gap.** The STE dictionary lists
`discontinue (v)` as not approved and names `STOP (v)` — `STOP THE TEST` against
`Discontinue the test`. Both of STOP's approved meanings are terminal: *to cause the end of a
procedure, movement, or operation*, and *to come to an end*. A discontinued word did not end.
Waytide stopped using it. `DISCARD (v)` is approved with the meaning *to not use again* and does
carry it, and the developer chose `discontinue` over it on 2026-08-08.

**`reserve` is a gap.** The word is absent from the STE dictionary, like `efferent`, `cradle`, and
`solubility`, so the standard never considered it. It was already in use at 13 places across five
packages, always meaning *keep this for that case only*. The declaration also covers the sense the
developer settled on 2026-08-08: **Waytide reserves the word, {X}** is the instruction that puts a
term into this set.

**The absent case and the not-approved case differ in what a declaration costs.** Absent is the
ordinary case for a coined term. Not approved means the standard considered the word and named
something else, so keeping it is a disagreement. Of the thirty-six declared terms, `discontinue` is
one of the few in the second case.

## Findings

1. The specification holds **53 rules** in nine sections. The guide to the writing rules gives the
   same count. The STE dictionary holds **1,898 distinct words**: 729 approved, 1,240 not approved,
   with 71 words carrying both an approved entry and one that is not approved, for different parts
   of speech.
2. Procedural sentences take a maximum of 20 words, by STE Rule 5.1. Descriptive sentences take a
   maximum of 25, by STE Rule 6.3.
3. A descriptive paragraph takes no more than six sentences, by STE Rule 6.6. STE Rule 6.5 allows each
   paragraph one topic. Section 5, procedural writing, has no paragraph rule.
4. Eight Waytide terms are approved in the STE dictionary and are used with the approved meaning.
   Two more carry an approved word with a meaning Waytide does not use, which STE Rule 1.3
   forbids: `loop`, where `LOOP (n)` is *a circular shape made in a flexible material*, and
   `mechanical`, where `MECHANICAL (adj)` is *related to or operated with a mechanism*.
5. Fourteen Waytide terms are not approved. Five of the fourteen have an approved word that carries
   the meaning.
6. Twenty-four Waytide terms are absent from the STE dictionary.
7. Category 19 covers Waytide's absent terms. The category is Computer science, information and
   communication technology.
8. STE Rule 1.8 requires technical nouns to be approved in the company or subject field. Each package's
   `vocabulary.md` does this.
9. `gate` breaks STE Rule 1.7 and STE Rule 1.13. It is settled as a technical noun, with WAIT for the verb.
   The noun holds 97 of its 132 uses across `system/`, so keeping the noun is the cheaper side as
   well as the one the package is built on.
10. `actuation` does not break STE Rule 1.7 or STE Rule 1.13. It breaks STE Rule 1.3 with the STE dictionary, which
   replaces it with OPERATION.
11. The OODA phase names `Observe`, `Orient`, `Decide`, and `Act` are none of them approved. OODA mode's
    artifact names rest on them.
12. STE Rule 3.5 governs the `-ing` form and permits it as a technical noun or as a modifier in a
    technical noun. The specification's examples are `Cleaning`, `Troubleshooting`, `switching
    relay`, `grinding wheel`, and `welding torch`. STE Rules 1.7 and 1.13 do not reach an `-ing`
    form.
13. STE Rule 2.1 permits a multi-word noun of no more than three words. Three is permitted.
14. Section 5, procedural writing, holds STE Rules 5.1 through 5.5 and has no paragraph rule. The
    six-sentence paragraph limit is STE Rule 6.6, in Section 6, descriptive writing.

## A published cheat sheet, checked against the specification

**A cheat sheet was supplied on 2026-08-08**, an HTML summary of the standard. It was checked
against the specification, which is the authoritative source and is in this repository.

| The cheat sheet says | The specification says |
|---|---|
| `6 sentences max — procedural paragraph` | STE Rule 6.6 is descriptive writing. Procedural writing has no paragraph rule |
| `No strings of 3+ nouns` | STE Rule 2.1 permits a multi-word noun of no more than three words |
| `No -ing forms as verbs or nouns (no gerunds)` | STE Rule 3.5 permits the `-ing` form as a technical noun or as a modifier in a technical noun |
| `One command per sentence. Split compound steps.` | STE Rule 5.2 excepts two or more actions that occur at the same time |
| `Keep technical names & verbs` | Issue 9 retired *technical name* for **technical noun** |

**What it gets right:** the 20-word and 25-word sentence limits, active voice, present tense, the
simple verb forms, one topic per paragraph, keeping articles, warning for people against caution for
equipment, and all ten of its word swaps.

**It supplied no fact the specification does not.** Its use here was to send a claim of the agent's
back to the specification, where the claim failed.

## Misses

Outcomes the forecast did not account for.

15. **The forecast used a term the specification retired.** It wrote *technical-name provision*.
   Issue 9 uses **technical noun**, and states the replacement: *The term "technical noun" fully
   replaces the previous term "technical name" in all parts of the document.* The phrase
   `technical name` appears nowhere in the document. The forecast was written from an account of an
   earlier issue.
16. **The forecast named the wrong term as the hard case.** It predicted `actuation` would test the
   provision's reach. `actuation` is an ordinary dictionary case: not approved, replaced by
   OPERATION, resolved by STE Rule 1.5. **The hard case is `gate`**, which the design-by-efferent
   vocabulary uses as a noun and as a verb, and which STE Rules 1.7 and 1.13 forbid. The agent
   attributed the STE Rule 1.7 and 1.13 case to `actuation` first, and the developer corrected it.
17. **The forecast did not predict agreement.** It predicted the STE dictionary is where the
   specification breaks against Waytide, and said nothing about terms that would match. **Ten
   Waytide terms are approved in the STE dictionary**, chosen by the `language` package's own standard
   before the STE dictionary was read.
