# Experiment — STE Spec

## Question

**What does the ASD-STE100 specification say?**

**The question is descriptive, and a forecast is what makes it testable.** A descriptive question
reaches no verdict on its own. This record forecasts the specification's structure and content
before the document is read. The gap between the forecast and the document is the finding. That
gap is what the verdict is about.

## Forecast

Committed before the document is read. Each item carries the agent's confidence.

1. **Two parts.** Part 1 is the writing rules. Part 2 is the dictionary. *High confidence.*
2. **The writing rules are numbered and grouped by subject** — words, noun clusters, verbs,
   sentences, procedures, descriptive writing, safety instructions, punctuation. *Moderate
   confidence on the grouping. Low confidence on the exact section names.*
3. **The rule count is between 50 and 70.** *Low confidence.*
4. **The dictionary is the larger part**, by a wide margin. *High confidence.*
5. **A dictionary entry carries the approved word, its part of speech, its approved meaning, an
   example of use, and the words it replaces.** *Moderate confidence.*
6. **Sentence length limits are stated as maximums**, and differ between procedural and descriptive
   writing. *High confidence that limits exist. Low confidence on the numbers.*
7. **A paragraph limit exists** for descriptive writing. *Low confidence.*
8. **Technical names and technical verbs may be added by a project.** *High confidence. This is the
   provision the parent experiment's second deferred item rests on.*
9. **The specification will contain rules Waytide already follows** — the one-meaning constraint,
   the ban on ambiguity, and consistent use of one word for one thing. *High confidence.*
10. **The specification will contain rules Waytide breaks throughout** — sentence length above all.
    Waytide's rule prose runs to sentences far beyond any plausible limit. *High confidence.*

## The vocabulary comparison

**Every Waytide vocabulary term was checked against the dictionary.** A lookup was built over the
1,898 distinct dictionary words. The terms came from the `Terms` sections and the substitution tables of the
five `vocabulary.md` files. Retired terms were removed from the set.

**How the dictionary marks a word.** An approved word is in capitals with a definition. An
unapproved word is in lower case with the approved word that replaces it. A word can also be
absent. Rule 1.1 permits an absent word only as a technical noun or a technical verb.

### The three outcomes

| Outcome | Count | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Approved | 10 | Usable as it is, with the dictionary's part of speech and meaning |
| Not approved | 14 | The dictionary names a replacement |
| Absent | 24 | Permitted only as a technical noun or a technical verb |

### Approved already

`examine`, `increment`, `cycle`, `loop`, `mechanical`, `name`, `activate`, `install packages`,
`out of scope`, `work session record`.

**Waytide selected these ten words before the dictionary was read.** The `language` package
selects the plain word that says the thing exactly. The dictionary approves the same ten words.

### Not approved, and an approved word carries the meaning

| Term | Dictionary replacement | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| `initiate` | START | Change. START says what Waytide means |
| `impact` | HIT | Change, but not to HIT. **EFFECT** is approved and carries the meaning |
| `convey` | MOVE | Change. MOVE says a value passed through layers |
| `protect` | PREVENT | Change. A test prevents a regression |
| `scenario` | absent; `case` replaced by CONDITION | Change to **CONDITION** |

### Not approved, and no approved word carries the meaning

`actuation` to OPERATION. `hinge` to TURN. `observe` to MONITOR. `orient` to POINT. `decide` to
SELECT. `conform` to AGREE. `product generation`, with no replacement given.

**The dictionary lists each of these words and names a substitute.** An absent word is not
listed.

### Absent

Twenty-four terms, of which the load-bearing ones are `efferent`, `solubility`, `cradle`,
`mean-bias`, `origination`, `deliberation`, `observation`, `controls`, `precondition`, `verified`,
`projection`, `reconcile`, `working-state artifact`, and `recognizable-content line`.

**Category 19 holds them.** The category is Computer science, information and communication
technology. Its own examples include AI, artificial intelligence, large language model, machine
learning, and prompt engineering. Rule 1.8 requires a technical noun to be approved in the company
or subject field. **Each package's `vocabulary.md` does that.**

### Settled on 2026-08-08, and the one term still open

1. **The OODA phase names.** `Observe`, `Orient`, `Decide`, and `Act` are all unapproved. They are
   quoted from Boyd. **OODA mode's artifact names, `orientation` and `action`, rest on them**, so
   this reaches the mode rule rather than one package's vocabulary.
2. **`gate`.** The design-by-efferent vocabulary uses it as a noun and as a verb, and says so
   outright. **Rule 1.7 forbids a technical noun used as a verb. Rule 1.13 forbids a technical verb
   used as a noun.** This is a rule violation, not a preference.
3. **`verified`.** The testing vocabulary requires it over `green`. `verify` is replaced by MAKE
   SURE, a verb phrase. `Verified: 47 tests` has no approved form, and the test-report format
   depends on that line.
4. **`conform`.** AGREE does not carry the meaning. Category 21 lists `conform to` as a technical
   verb for legal and regulatory texts, which Waytide's use is not.

### The eight terms settled on 2026-08-08

These terms stay as they are, declared under Rules 1.5 through 1.13 as technical nouns and
technical verbs. They join the terms already declared, and the whole declared set is the
**Waytide Technical Names**.

1. `Observe`, `Orient`, `Decide`, `Act` — the OODA phase names, quoted from Boyd.
2. `verified`
3. `conform`
4. `convey`
5. `scenario`

**`convey` and `scenario` were proposed for replacement and no longer are.** The proposals
were MOVE and CONDITION. Both are withdrawn, and both terms are now declared rather than
replaced.

**The label collides with a term the specification retired.** Issue 9 states that *technical noun*
fully replaces *technical name* in all parts of the document, and the phrase `technical name`
appears nowhere in it. **Waytide Technical Names** is Waytide's name for its own collection rather
than a claim about the specification's vocabulary, and it is recorded here as chosen.

**The OODA phase names are declared as nouns.** The design-by-efferent vocabulary names them as
phases in a table, and writes the verb forms in lower case — *the AI observes and acts*. Rule 1.7
forbids a technical noun used as a verb, so the lower-case verb forms are ordinary English rather
than uses of these four terms.

## Findings

1. The specification holds **53 rules** in nine sections. The guide to the writing rules gives the
   same count. The dictionary holds **1,898 distinct words**: 729 approved, 1,240 not approved,
   with 71 words carrying both an approved and an unapproved entry for different parts of speech.
2. Procedural sentences take a maximum of 20 words, by Rule 5.1. Descriptive sentences take a
   maximum of 25, by Rule 6.3.
3. A descriptive paragraph takes no more than six sentences, by Rule 6.6. Rule 6.5 allows each
   paragraph one topic. Section 5, procedural writing, has no paragraph rule.
4. Ten Waytide terms are approved in the dictionary.
5. Fourteen Waytide terms are not approved. Five of the fourteen have an approved word that carries
   the meaning.
6. Twenty-four Waytide terms are absent from the dictionary.
7. Category 19 covers Waytide's absent terms. The category is Computer science, information and
   communication technology.
8. Rule 1.8 requires technical nouns to be approved in the company or subject field. Each package's
   `vocabulary.md` does this.
9. `gate` breaks Rule 1.7 and Rule 1.13.
10. `actuation` does not break Rule 1.7 or Rule 1.13. It breaks Rule 1.3 with the dictionary, which
   replaces it with OPERATION.
11. The OODA phase names `Observe`, `Orient`, `Decide`, and `Act` are all unapproved. OODA mode's
    artifact names rest on them.

## Misses

Outcomes the forecast did not account for.

12. **The forecast used a term the specification retired.** It wrote *technical-name provision*.
   Issue 9 uses **technical noun**, and states the replacement: *The term "technical noun" fully
   replaces the previous term "technical name" in all parts of the document.* The phrase
   `technical name` appears nowhere in the document. The forecast was written from an account of an
   earlier issue.
13. **The forecast named the wrong term as the hard case.** It predicted `actuation` would test the
   provision's reach. `actuation` is an ordinary dictionary case: unapproved, replaced by
   OPERATION, resolved by Rule 1.5. **The hard case is `gate`**, which the design-by-efferent
   vocabulary uses as a noun and as a verb, and which Rules 1.7 and 1.13 forbid. The agent
   attributed the Rule 1.7 and 1.13 case to `actuation` first, and the developer corrected it.
14. **The forecast did not predict agreement.** It predicted the dictionary is where the
   specification breaks against Waytide, and said nothing about terms that would match. **Ten
   Waytide terms are approved in the dictionary**, chosen by the `language` package's own standard
   before the dictionary was read.
