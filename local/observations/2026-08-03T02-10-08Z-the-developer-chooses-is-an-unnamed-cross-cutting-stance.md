# "The developer chooses" is a cross-cutting stance that no rule names

**Status:** a working hypothesis under discovery. It records that a stance already present
across four packages and seventeen rules is stated as a stance in none of them, and that naming
it may be a rule in its own right. It proposes no change yet. It is promoted once it is decided
whether the stance is named, where such a rule lives, and whether naming it changes anything or
only makes it findable.

**The observation, as the developer put it:** the choose-your-own-adventure property "could even
turn out to be a rule that creates an overall personality trait that gives this property to
multiple surfaces of Waytide."

**It is not a candidate property — it is already there.** Seventeen rules across **four
packages** put a choice to the developer rather than deciding it:

| Package | Where |
|---|---|
| `design-by-efferent` | every hinge (hinge-cycle); the actuation gate's candidates; naming via options; the attended-or-unattended cadence; the implementation hinge's two candidates; **every prompt whatever its subject** (present-every-prompt) |
| `foundation` | the working location at a feature's and an experiment's initiation, with **no standing default**; branch deletion; the next deferred item, listed rather than picked; whether a session record is written, and the design reconciliation that follows it |
| `versioning` | the next version — patch, minor, or major — **never decided by the agent** |
| `code/ruby` | the release, which defers that choice to `versioning` |

**And one rule already forbids the opposite in general terms.** `present-every-prompt` requires
*every* prompt to go through the selection interface — not only hinge choices — and forbids
manufacturing cosmetic options to reach the two-option floor, on the ground that a fait accompli
puts the developer in judge mode. That is the stance applied to the *mechanism* of asking. What
is absent is a statement of the stance itself.

**The system has a rule for exactly this situation and has not applied it here.**
`rules-install-a-mindset-purpose-over-category` says a large share of rules exist to install a
**mental stance** rather than to standardize a mechanic, that purpose cuts across category, and
that a rule should state the mindset in its body and cross-reference siblings installing the
same stance through a different lever. Three files cite that rule. **None of the seventeen names
the stance they share.** A reader meeting any one of them sees a local convention; the stance is
visible only by reading all four packages and noticing.

**What naming it might buy:**

- **A test for new rules.** Where a rule would have the agent decide something, the stance asks
  whether it should be put to the developer instead — which is the question that was actually
  asked, ad hoc, at the working location, the version, the cadence, and the implementation
  hinge, each time from scratch.
- **A reason the apparatus exists.** The hinges, the selection interface, and the records of
  what was decided are one thing seen from three sides. Stated as a stance, they stop reading as
  three separate mechanisms.
- **The website's use.** It is already recorded as a point the website should make, where it
  explains why the system stops as often as it does — which otherwise reads as friction.

**What it might not buy, and the reason to be careful.** The stance is already **practiced
without being stated**, exactly as the citation-versus-dependency criterion was until it was
written down. That precedent cuts both ways: writing that one down changed no declaration and
was still worth doing, because it was discoverable only from an aside. But a stance rule that
merely restates what seventeen rules already do, without giving a test that decides a case they
do not cover, would be ceremony — the failure this system names most often.

**Where it would live.** `foundation` owns `rules-install-a-mindset`, and this would be a
sibling of it: a stance rule about the system's rules rather than about any surface.
`design-by-efferent` is where the stance is most concentrated but is the wrong home, since the
stance reaches `versioning` and `foundation` lifecycles that have nothing to do with the loop.

**What is not settled:** whether the stance is named at all; what test it would give that the
existing rules do not; whether "personality trait" is the right frame or whether it is simply
the method's premise stated once; and whether the analogy the developer used is admissible in
the rule, given that name-literally-not-by-analogy rejects analogies for naming a concept.
