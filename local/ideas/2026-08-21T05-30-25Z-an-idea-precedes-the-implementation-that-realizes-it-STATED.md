# An idea is recorded first, and a feature or experiment follows it at the engineer's pace

- **State:** Stated
- **Tags:** [observation]

**The observation, as the engineer stated it:** the process with Waytide works well when it is

1. Record an idea, then
2. Start a feature or experiment related to that idea

The feature or experiment could start immediately, or indefinitely. It is the engineer's choice.

## What each half has, and what the join has

**Each half is conventioned.** An idea has the ideas-convention, its eight states, and its log entry on creation and on each state change. An implementation has the implementations-convention and the implementation-lifecycle, with its branch, its working location, its declared end, and its own log entries.

**The relation between them is carried by neither.** No convention asks an implementation to name the idea it realizes, and none asks an idea to name its implementation.

**Where the sequence appears in the corpus.** Of the seventeen records in `local/implementations/`, three name a planning artifact, one of them written today. The observation is about how the sequence reads where it is used, and the count neither supports nor weakens it.

**`Realized` is the state on the idea's side.** The ideas-convention gives it as *what the idea describes exists*, which is what a completed implementation produces. Nothing connects the two records, so an idea's state moves by hand or not at all.

## Immediately or indefinitely

**"Immediately, or indefinitely" is what separates this from a pipeline.** An idea that waits is ordinary. `Open` and `Deferred` are both states an idea rests in, the `[deferred]` tag is the queue for work waiting its turn, and the queue is printed at every session's start so a waiting idea is not lost.

**So the sequence is an ordering rather than a schedule.** What it claims is that the idea comes first, not that the implementation comes soon.

## What it does not reach

**Work that settles no direction.** Correcting a stale package description, conforming addresses, and fixing a broken script each ran today with no idea ahead of them, and none wanted one. The observation is about work that has direction to settle. Reading it as a precondition on every change would make a record of every correction.

## What is not settled

- **Whether an implementation record should name the idea it realizes**, as a required line rather than as prose some records happen to carry.
- **Whether the idea's state moves when the implementation concludes**, and who moves it. A completed feature producing what an idea describes is the `Realized` case, and no rule fires there today.
- **Whether the relation is carried in one direction or two.** An idea that names its implementation goes stale when a second one supersedes the first. An implementation that names its idea does not.
- **Where it would be written.** The implementations-convention, the ideas-convention, or a rule of its own naming the join.

Related:

- the ideas-convention — the states, and `Realized` in particular
- the implementations-convention and the implementation-lifecycle rule — the half this joins
- the direction-and-sequencing-artifacts-take-a-log-entry and implementation-records-take-a-log-entry rules — the entries each half writes, which do not reference each other
- the deferred-queue-convention — the mechanism the indefinite case already has
- *Deferred items preempt design and plans* — the other record about how planning artifacts and the work relate, written when the repository held one design, one plan, and nine deferred items
