# Design and planning are process-centric; aspiration and intention are their human-centric corollaries, and a project could choose which mode it works under

- **State:** Stated
- **Tags:** [observation]

## The pairing

| Process-centric | Human-centric | What both answer |
|---|---|---|
| **design** | **aspiration** | what are we going toward |
| **plan** | **intention** | what are we going to do |

**The pairing is on a near/far axis, and testing the reverse confirms it.** Design and
aspiration are the further, less concrete of each pair; plan and intention the nearer.
Pairing plan with aspiration reads wrong for exactly that reason.

**The human-centric terms are looser than their counterparts, and that appears to be the
point rather than an imprecision.** A design carries dated **Settled** resolutions; an
aspiration settles nothing. A plan is a sequence of checkboxed tasks; an intention is a
commitment with no sequence in it.

## Where the distinction bites — the reason to think it is two modes and not two names

**Supersession may be unnecessary in the human-centric mode.** This is the sharpest of the
three. A plan that changes must be **superseded**: named in its successor's *Superseded plans*
section, its completed tasks kept as history, its authority explicitly transferred, because a
plan is authoritative and a reader must know which one governs. **Nobody supersedes an
intention.** An intention that changes has simply changed, and no record has been invalidated
by the change. If that holds, an entire apparatus — one this system built deliberately —
has no counterpart on the human-centric side.

**What non-completion means differs.** A plan task not done is **incomplete**, which is a
defect in the work or in the plan. An aspiration not reached is **still an aspiration**. The
process-centric artifact is measured against completion; the human-centric one may not be
measured at all.

**The gate moves.** A process-centric artifact is checked for **conformance** — does the
design carry its dated resolutions, does the plan state behavior rather than code. A
human-centric one would be checked against **the person's actual purpose** — is this what you
mean. The obligation moves from the document's shape to the stated intent, which is a
different kind of check and cannot be made mechanically.

## Waytide is not foreign to the human-centric stance

Design-by-efferent already centres the person: the human owns **Orient**, the gates exist to
provoke **origination** rather than ratification, and the escape is mandatory precisely
because the best answer is one the agent cannot produce. The proposal extends a stance the
system already holds inside its design loop outward, to the artifacts a project plans with.
That is an argument for its coherence, and not yet an argument that the second mode earns its
keep.

## Where the mode would be established

**Not the orchestrator, despite the initial supposition.** `read-consuming-projects.sh`,
settled 2026-08-04, is **discovery only** — it detects no status and takes no action on a
project — and it reaches projects that **already exist**. A mode chosen at initiation is not
its moment and writing into a project is not its job.

**The moment the system actually has is `install.sh`**, foundation's installer, which already
places the root `AGENTS.md`, the `CLAUDE.md` that imports it, and `.claude/settings.json`.

**But this exposes a gap: Waytide has no project-level initiation choice at all.** Chosen at
initiation, recorded as a confirmation, held for the duration — that pattern exists at the
**feature** and **experiment** level, for the working location and the attended-or-unattended
cadence. It has never existed at the **project** level. So the proposal is a structural
addition and not one more option in an existing gate.

**The definitions rest with Waytide**, as the developer put it, and that is consistent with
how the system is already arranged: `foundation` owns `local/design/` and `local/plans/`, so
it would own whatever the human-centric mode's artifacts are, or own what changes about those
two directories under it.

## What is not established

- **Whether it is two modes or one mode with looser obligations.** The decisive question. If
  *aspiration* and *intention* are what a design and a plan are **called** when a project
  declines the formal obligations, that is one axis with a switch on it — considerably cheaper
  than two parallel systems, and possibly the truer description.
- **The doubling cost.** Every rule governing `design/` and `plans/` would need a counterpart
  or a conditional. `foundation` holds roughly thirty rules and four of them are the document
  formats and directory conventions for these two artifacts. Forking them is the kind of cost
  this system refuses by habit — *ceremony installed where the justification does not reach*.
- **Whether separate directories are wanted** — `local/aspiration/` and `local/intention/`
  beside `design/` and `plans/` — or whether the same directories are worked differently under
  the chosen mode. Different answers, and the first is much the more expensive.
- **What a mixed project does.** Whether a project may hold a design and an intention at once,
  or whether the mode is exclusive for its duration as the working location and the cadence
  are.
- **Where `migration/` falls.** The third planning artifact has no obvious human-centric
  corollary, and a mode that covers two of three planning artifacts is not yet a mode.

## What was settled, 2026-08-04

The developer directed the feature that realizes this, and it answered most of the above — one
answer running **against** this observation's central argument, which is why the record is
reconciled here rather than left to read as though it had predicted the outcome.

- **It is two modes, with separate directories** — `local/aspiration/` and `local/intention/`.
  The observation named the two-modes question decisive and separate directories the more
  expensive answer; both went that way.
- **Formal is the default.** `local/migration/` is out of scope.
- **But the conventions and document formats are the same in both modes** — and that
  **abandons this observation's strongest argument**. *Nobody supersedes an intention* was the
  claim that made these two modes rather than two names; with shared conventions, an intention
  **is** superseded exactly as a plan is, and is measured by completion the same way. What the
  modes differ in is the **vocabulary and the directory**, and nothing else.
- **The justification therefore moved**, and the system supplies it: `language-is-precise-here`
  (a word is the thought's working surface) and `rules-install-a-mindset` (vocabulary is the most
  direct lever on a mental model). The mode installs a stance in the person while the mechanics
  stay identical. **This is a different claim from the one recorded above**, and the better one.
- **The mode does not change after initiation**, deferred for further consideration.
- **`install.sh` cannot issue the prompt**, as this observation anticipated — the mode is a rule
  the agent follows, and the feature is built on that.

Still open, and not answered by the feature: where `migration/` falls, and what a mixed project
would do.

## Its relation to the observation beside it

The **planning-spans-three-artifacts** observation asks whether the planning **activity** wants
a name, and warns against inventing a covering term vague enough to span design, implementation
plan, and migration record. This is a different move — a **parallel pair**, not an umbrella —
so that warning does not land on it directly. The underlying test does: does the new word do
work, or does it rename something that already had a name.
