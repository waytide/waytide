# Feature — The OODA mode

## Intent

Add a **third project mode**, **OODA**, alongside formal and intuitive. Its planning artifacts are
an **orientation** and an **action**, drawn from the OODA loop that the design-by-efferent
vocabulary already builds on.

The mode selection put at a project's initiation becomes three options rather than two. Nothing
about the two existing modes changes, and **the covering terms do not change** — they remain
**the direction artifact** and **the sequencing artifact**, settled in `b5d2ad8` and confirmed by
the OODA terminology experiment, which was refuted on exactly that question.

| Mode | Direction artifact | Sequencing artifact |
|---|---|---|
| formal (the default) | a design, in `waytide/local/design/` | a plan, in `waytide/local/plans/` |
| intuitive | an aspiration, in `waytide/local/aspiration/` | an intention, in `waytide/local/intention/` |
| **OODA** | **an orientation** | **an action** |

The two directories are `waytide/local/orientation/` and `waytide/local/action/`, named for their
artifacts as every existing planning directory is.

## Why a feature rather than an experiment

Nothing is under test. The mode is **built to an intent** — the developer has decided they want
it — where an experiment forecasts, reaches a verdict on a question, and merges through a test
gate on affirmation. This feature does none of those: it is verified before it integrates and
ends **completed**.

The distinction was drawn at the conclusion of the OODA terminology experiment, which established
that the proposal was two proposals — a generalization and a mode. The generalization was refuted.
The mode survives, and it survives as ordinary work rather than as an open question.

## What the experiment left for this feature, and how each was answered

Two forecast points went untested when the experiment reached its verdict. Both are now answered.

- **Whether two phases of four reads as an omission.** **Answered in the rule.** Neither Observe
  nor Decide produces a *planning* artifact — Observe is the agent surfacing state, shown rather
  than written down, and Decide happens at a gate and is recorded in the decision log and loop
  records, which are not planning artifacts and do not vary by mode. The mode takes the loop's two
  poles of intent and leaves its other two phases where they already live. The rule says so
  outright rather than leaving a reader to work it out.
- **Whether the trailing class noun holds under pressure.** **The question dissolved.** It assumed
  the OODA words would be the covering terms, which the experiment refuted. They are this mode's
  **concrete** vocabulary — *an orientation*, exactly as formal mode has *a design* — and the
  covering terms remain **the direction artifact** and **the sequencing artifact**, unrelated to
  any mode's words. There is no pressure on the class noun because the two are no longer the same
  thing.

## What was built

- **The mode rule renamed** to `a-project-works-in-a-mode-chosen-at-initiation`, its title with it,
  and 13 citations reconciled across 8 rule files, the foundation README, and `install.sh`. A name
  enumerating the modes goes stale on every mode added, which is the specificity the rule's own
  covering-term argument says an installed rule should not carry.
- **The mode rule extended to three modes** throughout — the table row, the near/far sentence, the
  local-rule template's third fill, the corroboration section generalized off the two modes it had
  named, and a new section on the OODA vocabulary.
- **The five rules that are *about* the artifacts** now name the OODA pair beside the other two,
  per the mode rule's own requirement.
- **The foundation README** follows.
- **The mode selection at initiation becomes three options**, formal still the default.

## Verification

No test suite exists in this repository. What was confirmed before integrating, on 2026-08-05: the
old rule name appears nowhere; no two-mode phrasing remains in `system/` outside
design-by-efferent's unrelated attended/unattended pair; each of the five artifact rules names the
OODA pair; no file carries duplicate consecutive provenance lines; every shell script parses.

## What this feature does not do

**It does not touch this repository's own mode.** This project works in intuitive mode and
continues to. Its local mode rule is a **dated snapshot** whose explanation binds nothing and is
not obliged to track the system rule, so its *"The two modes"* paragraph stays as written.

**It creates no directories.** `waytide/local/orientation/` and `waytide/local/action/` are named
by the rule and come into being in a project that chooses the mode, as every planning directory
does.
